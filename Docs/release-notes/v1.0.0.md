A charm hangs from your menu bar on a simulated rope. Nudge it and it swings,
carries momentum, and settles — because it is real physics, not a looping
animation.

This is the first public release.

## What it does

**Real rope dynamics.** Twenty Verlet segments solved at a fixed 240 Hz with
Gauss-Seidel constraint relaxation. No link stretches past 1.02× its rest length
under any input you can give it, and the rope behaves identically at 60 Hz,
120 Hz and ProMotion's variable rates. Grab the charm, throw it, and the momentum
you gave it is the momentum it keeps.

**Beads that are simulated too.** The beads above each charm are their own
particles riding the cord, with their own size, weight and spacing. They slide as
the rope whips and settle back where the artwork drew them.

**Sixteen charms.** Eleven from a collection of protective and lucky charms from
around the world — nazar boncuğu, hamsa, nimbu-mirchi, ghanta, drishti bommai,
pánchángjié, daruma, maneki-neko, horseshoe, scarab, himmeli — plus five
geometric classics. Each has its own mass, size and sound feeding the solver, so a
heavy bell hangs steeper than a straw ornament.

**Your own charms.** Drop any PNG, JPEG, WebP or HEIC onto the charm, or open the
Studio with ⌘N. It removes the background using Vision's foreground segmentation,
finds the subject, and hangs the result on the rope. Unlimited, with undo.

**A Charm Library** with search, categories, favourites, live previews and
one-click switching.

**Sound, if you want it.** Synthesized per-material — bell, metal, glass, wood,
soft — with a volume control. Nothing plays unless you moved something.

## Performance

| State | CPU | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| Settled | 0.6% of one core | 26 MB |
| Rope moving | ~15% of one core | 33 MB |
| Launch → on screen | ~210 ms | — |

The settled figure is where Hangly spends nearly all its life. When the rope stops
moving the solver stops working, nothing is published, SwiftUI never invalidates,
and the canvas is never asked to draw. There are no blur or shadow filters
anywhere in the render path.

## Privacy

Hangly collects nothing. No telemetry, no analytics, no tracking, no accounts, and
no network calls at all — the binary links no networking framework. Your settings
and any charms you import are plain files on your Mac.

It requests no privacy-protected resource: no camera, no microphone, no location,
no screen recording, no accessibility access.

## Installation

Download `Hangly.dmg`, open it, and drag Hangly to Applications.

**The first launch will be refused.** Hangly is not yet signed with an Apple
Developer ID, so macOS will say the developer cannot be verified. Right-click the
app and choose **Open**, then confirm — once. The entire source is in this
repository if you would rather build it yourself.

Hangly appears in your menu bar. There is no Dock icon and no window; that is
expected. A fresh install adds itself as a login item, which you can turn off in
Settings → General.

## Known limitations

- **Not notarised.** The Gatekeeper warning above is expected. Signing is the
  first item on the roadmap.
- **Apple Silicon only.** The build is `arm64`; Intel Macs cannot run it. A
  universal build is planned.
- **macOS 14 Sonoma or later**, because the app relies on `@Observable` and on
  `MenuBarExtra` behaviour introduced there.
- **One charm at a time.** One rope, one charm.
- **On macOS 26**, large icon sizes are drawn inside the system's icon container,
  which double-frames an icon that already has its own rounded background. Visible
  in Get Info; not in the Dock or Finder.
